## Inference (Generation) Hyperparameters

### Temperature

- `Temperature`
  - a **hyperparameter** that controls the **randomness and creativity** of the text generated by a large language model (LLM).
  - how it works:
    - LLMs do not write sentences all at once; they **predict the next token** (word or sub-word) based on a **probability distribution** derived from raw model scores called logits.
  - **Low Temperature**: T → 0
    - **Sharpens** the probability distribution, The model nearly always picks the top choice
    - results in **deterministic, repetitive, and focused outputs.**
  - **High Temperature**: T → 1.0 or higher
    - **Flattens** the distribution,
    - **increases variety, surprise, and creative** flair

---

### Top-P

- `Top-P` / `nucleus sampling`
  - an LLM generation parameter that controls **text randomness and creativity** by dynamically **filtering the pool** of next-token choices.
  - **Limits the percentage of candidates** to consider in generation.
  - how it works:
    - The model **considers the top P% of candidates**, where P is the value you choose.
    - Choose a higher value to consider **more candidates**.
  - **Lower Top-P** (e.g., 0.1 to 0.5):
    - Restricts the vocabulary to **safe, high-confidence, and predictable words**.
    - Best for code generation, data extraction, or **factual** Q&A.
  - **Higher Top-P (e.g., 0.9 to 1.0)**:
    - Allows a wider "nucleus" of less likely words, **increasing creativity, variety, and unexpected phrasing**.

---

- **Best Practice**:
  - Most platform guides recommend adjusting either `Temperature` or `Top-P` at a time, rather than heavily tweaking both simultaneously.

---

### Top-K

- `Top-K`
  - a hyperparameter in Large Language Models (LLMs) that **limits the model's next-token selection** to the fixed K most probable words or sub-words.
  - How It Works
    - **Limits the number of candidates** to consider in generation.
    - The model considers the **top K candidates**. Choose a higher value to consider more candidates.
  - **Low Top-K** (e.g., K = 1 to 10):
    - Acts more like a **greedy search**. It makes the model's output much more **deterministic, focused, and repetitive**.
  - **High Top-K** (e.g., K = 40 to 100+):
    - Expands the candidate pool, allowing **less common or more creative tokens** to be chosen, which increases variety.

---

### Work together

- **Using Them Together**
  - `Top-K` **limits choices** to the most probable tokens
  - `Temperature` controls **randomness** in predictions
    - **Low temperature**: **Safer**, more predictable text
    - **High temperature**: **More creative** and varied text
  - `Top-P` **selects tokens** based on cumulative **probability**

- **Work Together**
  - **1. The Raw Scores (Logits)**
    - generates a `raw score` (called a `logit`) for every word in its vocabulary based on prompt
    - **Higher** scores mean the model thinks that word **fits best**.
  - **2. `Temperature` Reshapes the Scores**
    - divides all the `raw scores` by the `temperature` value before they are turned into percentages.
  - **3. `Top-K` Slices the List by Count**
    - limits next-token candidates.
  - **4. `Top-P` Slices the Remaining List by Percentage**
    - Sorts tokens by probability and cuts off the list
  - **5. executes `Random Sampling`**
    - the **process of picking the next token** (word or subword) from a probability distribution
